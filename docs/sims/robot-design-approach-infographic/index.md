## About This MicroSim

Cozmo, Vector, Miko, and Buddy agreed on exactly one thing: a robot's face should be
a screen. Everything else went four separate directions, from tank treads to a full
navigating base, from toy shelves to classrooms. This infographic breaks each robot
into the same four design dimensions so you can differentiate them fairly. Your goal
is to contrast those choices with what happened to each product, and to notice that
the screen face is not what separated the survivors from the shutdowns.

## How to Use

1. Click any card to expand it. You will see that robot's face style, mobility,
   primary interaction mode, target buyer, and business outcome.
2. Hover over a robot's illustration to read a one-line caption naming its specific
   screen-as-face design detail.
3. Click **Compare Mobility** to show the mobility line on all four cards at once,
   from Cozmo's tank treads to Buddy's room-navigating base.
4. Click **Compare Outcome** to recolor each card's border by what happened to that
   product. A color key appears below the cards.
5. Click **Reset** to collapse every card and turn both comparison views off.

## Lesson Plan

### Grade Level
9-12 (High School)

### Duration
10-15 minutes

### Prerequisites

- Can name the four robots covered in this chapter: Cozmo, Vector, Miko, and Buddy.
- Understands that a screen-based face animates its expressions instead of moving
  physical parts.
- Knows that a product's target buyer shapes its price and its features.
- Has read the chapter timeline of when each robot launched.

### Activities

1. **Exploration** (5 min): Expand all four cards and copy the five fields into a
   comparison table of your own, one row per robot.
2. **Guided Practice** (5 min): Turn on **Compare Mobility** alone and rank the four
   robots from least mobile to most mobile. Then turn on **Compare Outcome** and
   check whether your ranking predicts the outcomes. It does not.
3. **Assessment** (5 min): Write two sentences naming the design dimension that best
   separates the surviving product from the discontinued ones, and cite the card
   evidence you used.

### Assessment

- Correctly fills in a four-by-five comparison table using only the cards.
- Names at least two design dimensions on which the four robots disagree.
- Explains why the shared screen-based face cannot by itself explain the different
  outcomes.
- Uses evidence from a specific card, not a general opinion, to support the claim.
