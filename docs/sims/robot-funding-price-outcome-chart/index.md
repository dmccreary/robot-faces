## About This MicroSim

This chart puts two numbers side by side for the same four robots: what each one cost, and how much money its company raised. The bar colors carry a third variable, the outcome, so you can compare the numbers against what actually happened. Your goal is to examine whether either figure predicted survival, and the honest answer is that neither one did. Test your own hypothesis first, then toggle to the Funding view and see whether the pattern you expected holds up.

All figures are approximate and rounded for teaching purposes. They are not exact financial records.

## How to Use

1. Start in the **Price** view and compare the four launch prices. Predict which robot you think survived.
2. Click **Funding** to swap the same four bars over to total money raised.
3. Hover over any bar to read the exact value plus a one-sentence note on that robot's outcome.
4. Click a bar to highlight its outcome in the color key at the top right.
5. Read the two italic callouts in the Funding view, then decide whether more money meant more success.

## Lesson Plan

### Grade Level
9-12 (High School)

### Duration
10-15 minutes

### Prerequisites

- Knowing the four robots and their release order, from the timeline MicroSim earlier in Chapter 2
- Knowing that Anki shut down in 2019 and that Vector was later revived, from Chapter 2
- Comfort reading a bar chart with a labeled vertical axis
- Understanding that "$200M" means two hundred million dollars

### Activities

1. **Exploration** (5 min): In the **Price** view, rank the four robots from cheapest to most expensive. Write down which one you predict is still being made today, and why.
2. **Guided Practice** (5 min): Switch to **Funding** and hover over every bar. Note that Cozmo and Vector share one value because both came from Anki, a single company. Which robot raised the most, and what happened to it?
3. **Assessment** (5 min): Click each bar to highlight its outcome in the color key. Then write two sentences answering the question: did a higher price or a larger funding total predict which robot survived?

### Assessment

- The student correctly reads at least three values from the chart in both views.
- The student states that Anki raised the most money, over $200 million, and still shut down.
- The student states that Miko raised far less and is still shipping new models.
- The student concludes that neither price nor funding predicted the outcome, and names at least one other factor, such as a clear audience or on-time delivery.
